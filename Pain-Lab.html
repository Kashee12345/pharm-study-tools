Part 1: Prostaglandin Bench

Pick a drug and watch where it lands. Arachidonic acid (the zigzag) is cut out of cell membranes and fed to two enzymes. COX-1 makes the housekeeping prostaglandins that protect the stomach, help platelets clump and keep blood flowing to the kidney. COX-2 makes the prostaglandins of an inflamed joint, pain and fever. Then move the clock to see which effects fade and which stay.

    Part 2: Opioid Safety Monitor

    Your patient had surgery this morning and rates the pain 8 out of 10. One opioid dose has just been given. Start the clock and watch the sedation level, breathing rate and oxygen saturation. Add a second dose, a second sedating drug or an older patient, and practise when to hold the opioid and when to give naloxone. Opioid amounts are relative teaching units, not doses.
